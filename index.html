01 / WHAT YOU PLAY

A familiar place.
An impossible connection.
A way back to learn.

Your objective is always the same: get home.

You cross a door and emerge somewhere else. When you try to go back, you do not return to the room you left. The next crossing may change your location, orientation, gravity, scale, time or world state.

Progress comes from understanding those relationships. You form a theory, test a route, and use what you learn to reach somewhere you could not reach before.

03 / HOW THIS BECOMES A PUZZLE

CONCEPTUAL ROUTE / NOT A FINAL LEVEL

Going back

A conventional map places one room beside another. This game connects separate captured places through a network of thresholds.

A crossing depends on the rules and state of the world. The same door can lead somewhere different. These relationships can be learned; they are not random.

04 / THE PUZZLE EXPANDS INTO SPACE

GENERATED CONCEPT / NOT GAMEPLAY

Look up.
That is a place

A street above you can have its own “down.”

After a crossing, your immediate surroundings may feel upright. Looking beyond them reveals that another place occupies a different orientation. Navigation means learning how these separate frames of reference connect.

Escher's Relativity is the spatial reference: multiple systems of gravity within one architecture. Here, that architecture becomes a navigation puzzle built from captured places.

05 / WHY ECUADOR

REAL LOCATIONS / PHOTOGRAPHIC RESEARCH

ONE COUNTRY.
MANY WORLDS.

Historic cities, volcanic highlands, rainforest and ocean. Ecuador gives each crossing a radical change of architecture, light, vegetation and scale. The contrast already exists. The game invents the connection.

THESE ARE PLACES TO BUILD FROM.

These selected documentary photographs establish the visual direction. They are not completed game captures. Fieldwork would focus on traversable fragments: a house, a courtyard, a workshop, a refuge and the space around its entrance.

ECUADOR IS THE FIRST UNIVERSE

A compact country.
A world of contrasts.

Ecuador brings extraordinary environmental and cultural variety into a relatively compact territory. It is the first source of places, people and spatial postcards for the game.

The system can grow beyond Ecuador. Captured places from other countries can become new nodes, new contrasts and new routes in the same expanding impossible geography.

06 / SPATIAL POSTCARDS

Capture the places.
Invent the geography.

A spatial postcard is a captured fragment of a real place.

We do not need to reconstruct every kilometre between Cuenca and Cotopaxi. We need meaningful spaces in each location, then playable connections between them.

A house, a refuge or a workshop provides a physical threshold. What happens after crossing it belongs to the game’s spatial rules. The surrounding territory does not have to be geographically continuous.

07 / FROM REALITY TO A PLAYABLE WORLD

Reality is
the raw material.

Unreal Engine is where the captured spaces would become a game: movement, collision, door connections, changes of orientation and puzzle state.

  1. 01

    Capture real Ecuador

    Photographs and video from cameras and drones document places, architecture and performances.

  2. 02

    Reconstruct the material

    Evaluate RealityScan / RealityCapture and Gaussian reconstruction workflows for spatial detail and game integration.

  3. 03

    Build in Unreal Engine

    Integrate visual captures with traversable geometry, collision, player movement and a door connection system.

  4. 04

    Make the impossible playable

    Connect separate spaces, define repeatable rules, and test whether players can understand and solve the route home.

3D GAUSSIAN SPLATTING / PLACES

Preserve the texture
of the real.

Image-based reconstruction can retain the irregular visual richness of a real location. The development challenge is to integrate that representation into a navigable, performant game world.

4D GAUSSIAN SPLATTING / PEOPLE

Capture a presence,
not just a place.

The proposed research extends capture into time and motion: an artisan working, a musician playing, someone walking. Real performances could inhabit the reconstructed spaces.

HERITAGE / TIME

One place.
More than one moment.

A later design direction connects a place to a reconstructed historical state of itself. Heritage becomes explorable memory; any historical reconstruction would be distinguished from a contemporary capture.
